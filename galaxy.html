Galaxies

What is a galaxy?

Definition

A galaxy is a huge system of stars, gas, dust, and dark matter, all held together by gravity. Galaxies can hold anywhere from a few million to several trillion stars, and they come in different shapes: spiral, elliptical, and irregular.

Types of galaxies

Astronomers group galaxies into three main shapes. Spiral galaxies have curved arms winding out from a central bulge. Elliptical galaxies, like NGC 1132 shown here, are smooth, rounded, and made mostly of old stars. Irregular galaxies have no defined shape at all, often the result of collisions or gravitational tugging from nearby galaxies.

How many galaxies are there?

Deep-sky images like the Hubble Ultra Deep Field, where nearly every point of light is an entire galaxy, suggest the observable universe contains somewhere between hundreds of billions and a couple of trillion galaxies.

Examples

What does the "M" mean?

Several galaxies below are labeled with an "M" followed by a number, like M31 or M104. That's the Messier designation: a catalog of bright nebulae, star clusters, and galaxies first compiled by French astronomer Charles Messier in the 1770s, originally so comet hunters wouldn't mistake them for comets. Today astronomers still use it as a quick, common way to refer to these objects.

Milky Way

Our own galaxy, a spiral galaxy that's home to the Solar System. Seen from above, it would look like a giant pinwheel with curved arms of stars.

Diameter: about 100,000 light-years. Mass: around 1.5 trillion solar masses, including dark matter.

Andromeda (M31)

The closest spiral galaxy to the Milky Way, about 2.5 million light-years away. On a dark, clear night it can even be seen with the naked eye as a faint smudge of light.

Diameter: about 152,000 light-years. Mass: roughly 1 trillion solar masses.

Triangulum (M33)

A smaller spiral galaxy than Andromeda, but packed with regions where new stars are constantly being born.

Diameter: about 60,000 light-years. Mass: between 10 and 40 billion solar masses.

Whirlpool Galaxy (M51)

A grand-design spiral about 31 million light-years away, famous for its clearly defined arms. It's actively interacting with a smaller companion galaxy, whose gravity is helping shape those bright spiral arms.

Diameter: about 76,000 light-years. Mass: around 160 billion solar masses.

Sombrero Galaxy (M104)

Seen almost edge-on, its bright core and thick ring of dust give it the look of a wide-brimmed hat. It holds an unusually large number of globular clusters, around 2,000 of them.

Diameter: about 50,000 light-years. Mass: around 800 billion solar masses.

Large Magellanic Cloud

A small irregular galaxy orbiting the Milky Way as one of its satellites, visible to the naked eye from the Southern Hemisphere. It's home to the Tarantula Nebula, one of the most active star-forming regions known.

Diameter: about 32,000 light-years. Mass: around 10 billion solar masses (about 138 billion including dark matter).

What is a galaxy made of?

Stars

Billions of stars of every size, color, and age make up a galaxy, often grouped into clusters like the Pleiades, shown here.

Gas and dust

Vast clouds of gas and dust, like the Pillars of Creation, are the nurseries where new stars are born.

Central black hole

Most galaxies have a supermassive black hole at their center. This is the first-ever direct image of one, taken in the galaxy M87, a giant elliptical galaxy about 132,000 light-years across with a total mass of roughly 2.7 trillion solar masses.

Dark matter

An invisible substance that doesn't emit or reflect light, but makes up most of a galaxy's mass. Its gravity is what keeps stars from flying off as galaxies spin, and its presence around clusters like Abell 1689 can even bend the light of galaxies behind them.
