Black holes

Cygnus X-1

The first object widely accepted by astronomers as a black hole. It orbits a blue supergiant star, pulling gas from it into a disk so hot it glows in X-rays.

Type: Stellar-mass

Mass: ~21 M☉

Event horizon: ~124 km across

Distance: ~7,000 light-years

M82 X-1

Sits in the rare gap between stellar and supermassive black holes. Objects this size are hard to find, and M82 X-1 is one of the best candidates for the class.

Type: Intermediate-mass

Mass: ~400 M☉

Event horizon: ~2,360 km across

Distance: ~12 million light-years

Sagittarius A*

The quiet giant at the center of our own galaxy. Stars have been tracked looping around it for decades, and in 2022 the Event Horizon Telescope finally imaged its shadow directly.

Type: Supermassive

Mass: ~4.3 million M☉

Event horizon: ~24 million km across

Distance: ~26,000 light-years

M87* — Pōwehi

The first black hole ever photographed, in 2019. It sits at the core of the galaxy Messier 87, and its shadow alone is wider than our entire solar system.

Type: Supermassive

Mass: ~6.5 billion M☉

Event horizon: ~38 billion km across

Distance: ~55 million light-years

TON 618

One of the most massive black holes ever measured, powering a quasar brighter than the entire Milky Way. Light would take weeks to cross its shadow.

Type: Ultramassive

Mass: ~66 billion M☉

Event horizon: ~388 billion km across

Distance: ~18.2 billion light-years
