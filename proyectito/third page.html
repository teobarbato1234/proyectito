Stars

The Sun

Diameter: ~1,392,000 km

Our own star, a yellow dwarf about 4.6 billion years old that holds the whole Solar System together with its gravity. It turns hydrogen into helium in its core at 15 million °C, and that energy takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth as light and heat.

Sirius

Diameter: ~2,380,000 km (1.7× the Sun)

Known as the Dog Star, it's the brightest star in the night sky and sits just 8.6 light-years from Earth. Sirius is actually a binary system: a bright main star and a tiny, super dense white dwarf companion called Sirius B, once part of Sirius A itself.

Betelgeuse

Diameter: ~970,000,000 km (700× the Sun)

A red supergiant marking Orion's shoulder, so huge that if it replaced the Sun it would swallow the inner planets out past Mars. It's around 640 light-years away, nearing the end of its life, and could go supernova within the next 100,000 years, astronomically speaking any moment.

Rigel

Diameter: ~108,700,000 km (78× the Sun)

A blue supergiant that lights up Orion's foot from about 860 light-years away. It shines roughly 66,000 times brighter than the Sun and is one of the most luminous stars visible to the naked eye, with two fainter companion stars orbiting nearby.

Polaris

Diameter: ~52,200,000 km (37.5× the Sun)

The North Star sits almost exactly above Earth's axis, so it barely seems to move, which is why sailors have used it to navigate for centuries. It's actually a triple star system about 430 light-years away, and its main star is a bright yellow supergiant.

Vega

Diameter: ~3,300,000 km (2.4× the Sun)

The fifth brightest star in the night sky, only 25 light-years away, and a corner of the Summer Triangle. It spins so fast that it bulges at its equator, and thanks to Earth's slow wobble, Vega was the pole star thousands of years ago and will be again in about 12,000 years.

Proxima Centauri

Diameter: ~214,000 km (0.15× the Sun)

The closest star to the Sun, a faint red dwarf about 4.2 light-years away, part of the Alpha Centauri system. It's small and dim compared to the Sun, but hosts at least one known rocky planet, Proxima b, orbiting inside its habitable zone.

Antares

Diameter: ~950,000,000 km (680× the Sun)

A giant red supergiant in Scorpius, about 550 light-years away, whose name means "rival of Mars" because of its fiery reddish glow. It's one of the largest and brightest stars known, with a hot blue companion star, Antares B, orbiting far out from it.
